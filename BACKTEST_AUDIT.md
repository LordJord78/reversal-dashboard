# Audit — `engine/backtest.py`

**Date:** 2026-08-04 · **Commit:** `f8a7f88` · **Method:** line review, clean-room re-implementation, statistical reproduction, sensitivity testing

---

## Verdict

The engine is sound. Every published statistic reproduces exactly, the replay logic
contains no look-ahead, and an independent re-implementation agrees trade-for-trade.

The problems are not in the arithmetic. They are in one latent indexing bug, a missing
test the code claims to have, and — most importantly — a gap between what this engine
computes and what the research note claims that the code attributes to the wrong cause.

---

## What was verified

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Look-ahead in the volatility window | **None.** `hist = sess[i-60:i]` is strictly prior to signal session `i`; the trade executes at `i+1`. Everything used is known before the entry. |
| Signal/trade session alignment | Correct. `i` fires, `i+1` trades, open-to-close, no overnight hold. |
| Sharpe calculation | Correct, including the analytic folding of sat-out sessions into mean and variance. Verified against a materialised zero-padded series. |
| Published statistics | **42 of 42 reproduce exactly** across all six tickers — Sharpe, gross Sharpe, avg bp, hit rate, total return, max drawdown, worst trade. |
| Clean-room replay | Trade sets match exactly for QQQ, DIA, SPY, TLT, GLD over the 520-session overlap. |
| Price adjustment | Unadjusted OHLC is the correct choice — dividends and splits affect close-to-close, not the intraday open-to-close move this rule trades. |
| Partial-bar contamination | Immune. `range(VOL_WINDOW, len(sess)-1)` never scores the final bar. This is why it disagreed with `signals.py` on 2026-08-04 and was right to. |

## Findings

### 1. `sess` and `rows` can silently fall out of alignment — HIGH (latent)

```python
sess = [(d, c / o - 1) for d, o, c in rows if o > 0]   # filtered
...
d, o, c = rows[i + 1]                                   # unfiltered, same index
"sd": rows[i][0],
```

`sess` is filtered by `o > 0`; `rows` is not. `i` indexes `sess` but is used to subscript
`rows`. Drop one bar and every subsequent index refers to a different session.

Demonstrated with a 70-bar synthetic series containing a single `open == 0` row:

```
clean series : (fired 2020-03-13, traded 2020-03-14)
one bad bar  : (fired 2020-03-12, traded 2020-03-13)
```

Same event, shifted a day. The trade is booked against the wrong session's open and close.
Nothing raises, nothing logs, and the output looks entirely plausible.

Currently dormant — no ETF bar in the current history has `open <= 0`. But the filter exists
precisely because bad rows were anticipated, and the day one arrives the backtest quietly
misattributes trades. Depending on direction of shift it can also become genuine look-ahead.

**Fix:** filter once, keep a single aligned structure.

```python
rows = [r for r in rows if r[1] > 0]
sess = [(d, c / o - 1) for d, o, c in rows]
```

`signals.py::compute()` has the same latent pattern where `price` reads `rows[-1][2]`
against a filtered `sess`.

### 2. The gap with the research note is not a cost-model artifact — MEDIUM

The module docstring says the cost model is a "plainer stand-in" and to treat a widening
gap against the note "as a finding, not as noise." Correct instinct, wrong attribution.
Costs are not capable of producing this gap:

| Ticker | Note SR (net) | Engine gross SR | Engine net SR | Cost drag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| QQQ | 0.60 | 0.722 | 0.716 | 0.006 |
| XLK | **0.31** | **0.720** | **0.705** | 0.014 |
| DIA | 0.35 | 0.461 | 0.449 | 0.012 |
| SPY | 0.38 | 0.446 | 0.436 | 0.010 |
| TLT | −0.17 | −0.110 | −0.160 | 0.050 |
| GLD | −0.09 | −0.263 | −0.309 | 0.046 |

The entire cost model moves Sharpe by ~0.01. **Even at zero cost the engine cannot reach
the note's numbers** — gross alone beats the note by 0.12 on QQQ and by 0.41 on XLK.

XLK is the case that matters. The dashboard prints `SR 0.31 · ex-crisis 0.17` from the note
directly beside a signal generated by an engine that measures the same instrument at 0.705 —
more than double. Both cannot be right. The same pattern holds ex-crisis: recomputed 0.658
against the note's claimed 0.51 for QQQ.

Costs are excluded as the cause. Remaining candidates: different sample period, different
data vendor, or a rule variant that differs from the one implemented here. Worth resolving,
because the tier system — which name is tradeable and which is only watched — rests on the
note's rankings, and this engine does not reproduce them.

---

### 3. `test_backtest.py` does not exist — MEDIUM

The docstring states:

> "The two must agree on the most recent session or one of them is wrong; test_backtest.py asserts that."

There is no `test_backtest.py`, and no test file anywhere in the repository. `engine/` contains
`backtest.py` and `signals.py` only.

That assertion is exactly the check that would have caught the 2026-08-04 partial-bar failure
on the day it was introduced, rather than after phantom 2σ signals had been published and
written into `history.json`. The code describes a safety net that was never built.

---

### 4. Returns are extremely concentrated — MEDIUM (disclosure, not a defect)

QQQ, 416 trades over 27.4 years:

| | share of total net return |
|---|---|
| Top 1 trade | 12.1% |
| Top 5 | 38.4% |
| Top 10 | 56.1% |
| **Top 20** | **80.2%** |

Twenty sessions out of 6,893 produce four-fifths of the result. Excluding 2008/2009/2020
removes 50 trades and 15.7% of return, leaving SR 0.658 — so this is not purely a crisis
artifact, but the outcome still rests on roughly twenty days in nearly three decades.

The README's honesty section covers walk-forward SR, max drawdown and the 27-year
confirmation horizon. This belongs alongside them: 416 trades sounds like a large sample
and, for the purpose of establishing this edge, it is not.

---

### 5. `max_drawdown` measures from the first trade, not from initial capital — LOW

```python
peak, worst = curve[0], 0.0
```

`curve[0]` is equity *after* the first trade. If that trade loses, the drawdown from
1.0 into it is invisible. Should be `peak = 1.0`.

Verified: currently zero effect on all six tickers — the published max drawdowns are already
correct. Latent only.

---

### 6. `SLIPPAGE_BP = 0.2` is optimistic for market-on-open / market-on-close fills — LOW

Breakeven slippage (SR → 0), charged per unit of position on top of the spread:

| Ticker | SR @ 0.2bp | @ 2bp | @ 5bp | @ 10bp | Breakeven |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| QQQ | 0.716 | 0.684 | 0.630 | 0.540 | **39.1bp** |
| XLK | 0.705 | 0.671 | 0.613 | 0.516 | 36.0bp |
| DIA | 0.449 | 0.392 | 0.297 | 0.139 | **14.3bp** |
| SPY | 0.436 | 0.383 | 0.295 | 0.147 | 14.9bp |

QQQ tolerates roughly 39bp before the edge disappears, which is a comfortable margin against
realistic MOO/MOC slippage. DIA and SPY die at ~14bp — thin enough that execution quality
alone decides whether they work. Reassuring that QQQ is the only tier-1 name; worth keeping
in mind before promoting anything out of the watch tier.

---

### 7. Threshold is knife-edge at the boundary — INFO

The clean-room replay reproduced XLK's trade list exactly except for one session:
2025-08-25 fired at **z = 2.002**. Recomputed from prices rounded to cents, it fell below
2.0 and vanished.

A trade decided in the third decimal is inherent to a hard threshold, not a bug. But it
means near-boundary fires are sensitive to data vendor and rounding, and XLK — lowest priced
and highest vol in the universe — is the most exposed.

---

### 8. First 60 sessions counted as flat in the Sharpe denominator — INFO

`n_sessions = len(rows)` includes the initial volatility window, during which the rule could
not have traded. Understates mean and Sharpe by ~0.9% on QQQ. Conservative, therefore fine —
noted only so it is not mistaken for an error later.

## Recommended order

1. Write `test_backtest.py` — assert `signals.py` and `backtest.py` agree on the most recent
   common session. It is named in the docstring, and it is the test that catches this class
   of failure. **(#3)**
2. Fix the `sess`/`rows` alignment in both engines. Small, silent, and unbounded. **(#1)**
3. Resolve the note-vs-engine discrepancy, starting with XLK. The tiering depends on it. **(#2)**
4. Add concentration to the README's honesty section. **(#4)**
5. Initialise `max_drawdown` peak at 1.0; raise `SLIPPAGE_BP` to something defensible and
   republish. **(#5, #6)**
