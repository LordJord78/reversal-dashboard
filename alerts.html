An alert is a session where the rule triggered. The previous session moved open→close by at least 2 standard deviations of its own 60-day range — the condition this strategy trades. Each row below is one trigger: in at that session's open, out at its close, same day. No overnight hold.

Nothing is sent anywhere. This is a record of when the rule fired, rebuilt from the same engine that produces the daily signal — not a notification feed. Want alerts actually delivered to email or your phone? That's a separate build; ask and I'll wire it up.

Control fires are normal. z is scaled by each instrument's own volatility, so TLT and GLD trigger about as often as anything else — roughly 12–15 times a year. They are controls because trading them earns nothing (SR −0.17 and −0.09 over the full history), not because they stay quiet. They're listed here, marked do not trade, and excluded from every performance figure. The thing that would actually signal a broken pipeline is a control developing real edge — check that on its backtest page, not from a handful of recent fires.

How to read a row
Fired is the session whose move triggered the rule. Traded is the next session, when the position was actually held — that gap is deliberate, since you can't act on a close until the following open.
Entry and exit are that traded session's open and close: the fills the rule assumes. O→C is how the instrument itself moved. Net is what the position returned after costs — it flips sign against O→C on shorts, which is the point of the strategy.
Size is leverage, not shares: 2.00x means twice account equity, capped there.
